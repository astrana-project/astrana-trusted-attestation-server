## Notes

- Every relationship in this set uses its own distinct Ed25519 keypair, per decision record 3. None are shared, even
  where one persona (Judy, Tara) holds relationships at two different organisations, or two relationships at the same
  one.
- Frank's and Grace's scenarios should be tested against `POST /attest` directly to confirm the returned `status` field
  reads `revoked` and `expired` respectively, not a bare `false`.
- Heidi's scenario should be tested with the organisation's Astrana Trusted Attestation server taken offline during a
  periodic re-check, to confirm Astrana treats it as unreachable and indeterminate rather than invalidating the
  connection.
- Judy's scenario requires two separate Astrana Trusted Attestation deployments running concurrently (different
  organisations). Tara's requires only one, but two relationship rows within it. Both are worth testing, because they
  exercise different parts of the design (cross-organisation and same-organisation selective disclosure).
- Across all personas, each of the thirteen IAM providers in the table is exercised at least once.
- **All 25 relationship types (decision record 14) are covered.**
- Zoe's scenario is the only locale mismatch, rather than a locale that happens to be supported. Use it when testing the
  fallback-disclosure requirement specifically.
- Xavier, Dave, and Quinn together form a three-way test of `resident` and `citizen`. The same field carries two
  different senses (civic and property-level), and `citizen` is an adjacent but distinct type.
- Sara and Tamar test right-to-left scripts (Persian, Hebrew). Mei, Nikos, Somchai, and Jin extend script coverage
  further (Chinese, Greek, Thai, Hangul), so the self-service UI is exercised against Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic,
  Hebrew, CJK, Hangul, and Thai scripts.
- **Every country has exactly six personas, evenly split three male and three female** by name, so the gender balance is
  exact per country, not only roughly even overall. Within a country, the personas differ in relationship type and
  organisation where possible, so no jurisdiction and locale combination is tested against only one relationship type or
  organisation.
